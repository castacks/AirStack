# Mathematical foundations

**E1.** For each wavelength or narrow effective channel, let ray distance s increase from the boundary toward the camera. With homogeneous extinction κ [m⁻¹] and constant source radiance S, dL/ds = −κL + κS. With boundary L(0)=L₀, optical depth τ=κd and transmission t=exp(−τ), the solution is L(d)=tL₀+(1−t)S. At zero distance or extinction L=L₀. As τ tends to infinity L tends to S. RGB S represents prescribed effective in-scattering and assumes a constant incident illumination field along each path. It may depend on channel. An absorbing path with no emission or scattering into the beam uses S=0. Directional scattering and multiple scattering are outside this approximation.

**E2.** The ray in camera coordinates is (x,y,1), with x=(u−cₓ)/f and y=(v−cᵧ)/f. Its intersection at z has point (zx,zy,z) and slant range z√(1+x²+y²). An opaque foreground intersection uses foreground material and truncates atmospheric distance. The target visibility mask is zero at those pixels. This mask represents geometric visibility only.

**E3.** Planck spectral radiance is Bλ(T)=2hc²/[λ⁵(expm1(hc/(λkT)))]. The wavelength is in metres, T in kelvin and Bλ in W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹·m⁻¹. Multiplying by 10⁻⁶ converts the density to per micrometre. The opaque diffuse gray surface boundary is εBλ(Tsurf)+(1−ε)Bλ(Trefl), with ε in [0,1]. The reflected term assumes isotropic blackbody-equivalent environment radiance. The LWIR slab assumes isothermal local thermodynamic equilibrium, negligible scattering and constant absorption with wavelength. Its source is Bλ(Tair). Thermal crossover is possible when the response-integrated radiances of two surfaces coincide, including reflection and path effects.

**E4.** The LWIR sensor proxy integrates dimensionless response R(λ) times Lλ over [8,14] µm using 128 uniform composite trapezoids. This gives W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹. RGB channel values are supplied effective band radiances in the same integrated units. The integral is unnormalized and uses an illustrative response ramp 0.8 to 1.0. Planck implementation supports 1–100 µm and 100–2000 K. The baseline integration uses 288–310 K. For these smooth spectra, halving the grid spacing should reduce the trapezoid error by approximately four.

**E5.** Observed radiance is truth plus additive zero-mean Gaussian noise with configured σ in the same units. Independent modality streams use fixed seeds. Shared case seeds deliberately hold noise realizations fixed during paired condition comparisons. The simulator performs no noise clipping, encoding, gain, quantization or conversion to counts. The physical nonnegativity constraint applies to noiseless radiance.
