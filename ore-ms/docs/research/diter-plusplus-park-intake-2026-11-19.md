# DiTer++ PARK natural-transition intake

The existing [Redcourse intake](redcourse-pose-reference-intake-2026-11-18.md)
and [archive inventory](exposed-data-adequacy-findings-2026-11-03.md) leave
Redcourse GNSS quality and camera-frame binding unresolved. The previously
reported IRS RTVI replay packet is unavailable in the current workspace.

DiTer++ PARK is a candidate for testing recovery through a recorded light
transition. The authors describe changing illumination and an underground
area around a campus building. Both robots record forward RGB, thermal, and
IMU streams. The supplied metric trajectory is generated by LiDAR scan-to-map
registration against a Leica RTC360 survey map, with inertial deskewing and
factor-graph localization. DiTer++ can provide a reference independent of
the image streams, with possible shared IMU inputs. Day and night involve
different robots and sensor configurations, so a comparison across them
would confound illumination with rig and route. [Author paper, Sections III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05839v1).

## Release and reference check

The author share lists full day and night ROS bags, a camera-free day bag,
two pose files, a reference point cloud, and a calibration document. The
calibration preview includes RGB and thermal intrinsics and an RGB–LiDAR
transform. It describes using the Unitree URDF for the kinematic chain.
Per-agent transforms and clock semantics still need a bag-level check.
[Pinned release repository](https://github.com/sparolab/DiTer-plusplus/tree/884384764e45e681252a305f862cc6de3a9f5195),
[author dataset share](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fo/zoawkn9s3thsjv3mkqux8/AN9G8TzeE_Uw92QBpE8hIKk?dl=0&rlkey=h3d9eu8pf0g6w3h0ywbdbqiv6).

Validated HTTP 206 ranges supplied 4 KiB of each bag header and the
complete connection and chunk summary index. The two full bag recordings
cover these ROS record-time intervals in Unix seconds.

Both references fall inside the daytime bag span and outside the nighttime

## Sparse camera scout

At 13 fixed timestamps spaced 120 seconds apart, bounded bag ranges
returned both image streams. The RGB stream reports `rgb8`, 640 × 480,
and `camera_color_optical_frame`. The thermal stream reports `mono16`,
640 × 512, and `boson_camera`. The sampled RGB median pixel intensity
ranges from 79.3 to 156.0 on an 8-bit scale. This cadence is too sparse
to establish the onset, duration, or recovery of an illumination change.
It also cannot establish physical synchronization from header timestamps.
The fixed grid and observed frame metadata are preserved in
`day-sparse-camera-scout.json`. No estimator was run.

## Passage-to-daylight follow-up

Before opening additional frames, select the lowest-RGB-median timestamp
from the 13-point scout, Unix second 1725610800. Sample every two seconds
in its fixed ±30 s window. Inspect full RGB frames and measure the median
RGB channel mean and the fraction at or below 32/255 on an 8 × 8 pixel
sampling grid. These are encoded image values, with no photometric
calibration or exposure metadata assumed.

All 31 RGB frames are present. The robot approaches an already visible
exit from a shaded passage and reaches the exterior around +8 to +10 s
(1725610808–1725610810). The sampled RGB median is 83.0 at the center,
115.7 at +8 s, and 141.7 at +10 s. The fraction at or below 32/255
peaks at 8.5%, at −10 s. The passage contains visible scene detail even
near the brightness minimum. The outdoor reveal changes scene geometry
and visible content along with illumination. Use this exposed interval
for a shaded-to-daylight field comparison. Tracking loss and recovery
require a separate measurement, and this scout did not inspect entrance
to the passage.

The full window contains 2,412 complete bag chunks with 1,798 RGB,
1,799 thermal, and 29,989 `/agent_os/imu/data` messages. Counts are from
chunk indexes on bag record time. They establish stream presence across
the window, with header-level continuity and synchronization still to be
checked. Both pose files bracket the interval with 602 rows apiece
and a maximum gap of approximately 100.59 ms. The TUM positions traverse
43.92 m along the sampled path. Reference-frame and sensor-clock
qualification remain open.

Thermal image payloads were retrieved at 28 of the 31 scout targets,
including the passage exit. Three requests at +22, +28, and +30 s returned
HTTP 200 without a byte-range response, leaving those targets without a
verified thermal frame. The 28 sampled thermal-minus-RGB header
differences span −11.4 to +55.1 ms. The two pose exports agree within
0.5 µs and 0.9 µm in the window, consistent with rounded copies of the
same trajectory.

The contact sheet, 31 RGB frames, and per-frame metrics and SHA-256 hashes
are under `/root/ore-ms-artifacts/diter-park/`. No estimator was run.

## Message timing and calibration preflight

Inspect exact ROS message headers in three windows, using whole chunks
that cover each window with 200 ms of record-time padding. The bounded
ranges total 340,524,601 bytes and their concatenated SHA-256 hashes
reproduce. Counts below use half-open intervals in header time.

| Window relative to scout center | RGB / thermal / external IMU | Nearest thermal minus RGB, median | Absolute difference ≤10 ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| +5 to +6 s | 31 / 30 / 500 | +3.20 ms | 31/31 |
| +8 to +10 s | 60 / 60 / 1,000 | +2.06 ms | 59/60 |
| +12 to +13 s | 30 / 30 / 500 | −14.98 ms | 1/30 |

All 121 RGB headers find a distinct nearest thermal header within
16.7 ms when searching the padded data. The sign and size of the
difference vary by window. RGB record time follows its header by
26.10–46.89 ms, versus 0.29–0.66 ms for thermal. For this diagnostic
use per-topic header time, and retain record time separately. The
observations do not determine physical exposure time or establish
hardware synchronization. All image headers match their respective
camera-info headers exactly. No duplicate or reverse image or IMU
headers occur within the three windows.

The bag's thermal camera info reports `fx=767.94` pixels for
640 × 512, while the standalone calibration preview lists a thermal
`fx` of about 513.38 pixels. The preview also lists an RGB–LiDAR
transform and points to a Unitree URDF for the base frame. The
bag index contains no TF topics. Neither reference file names its
tracked frame, and the release descriptions do not bind it to a
camera or IMU frame. A metric replay needs recording-specific
calibration provenance and an explicit reference-frame transform.

At the 37 adjacent reference intervals fully inside these windows,
compare the magnitude of reference rotation with each gyro's
trapezoidally integrated rotation over the same header timestamps.
No clock, bias, or axes are fitted. Median absolute angle discrepancy
is 0.316° for the external IMU and 0.353° for the LiDAR IMU, with
maxima of 2.599° and 2.656°. The median reference rotation is 1.174°.
This scalar test does not identify the axes, frame origin, or a
suitable clock correction. PARK remains usable for field frontend
characterization. Metric recovery scoring remains unqualified.

Raw ranges, parsed headers, timing summaries, and interval-level
rotation checks are under
