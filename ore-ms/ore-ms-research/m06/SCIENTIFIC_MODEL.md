# M06 shared RGB–LWIR forward simulation

The shared state consists of a world scene, atmospheric coefficient field, material thermal properties, illumination and detector settings. One frame has one elapsed timestamp t and one environment realization. A 9×7 ideal M04 pinhole with coincident RGB and LWIR axes shares the first-hit ray, target-only support, world point, slant range and forward camera depth. The world has +x right, +y image down and +z forward at the camera. A target at z=10 m stands before an extended z=15 m background. Opaque static and moving foreground patches sit at z=5 m. The material and geometry definitions are in the frozen configuration. This is synthetic co-registration with zero commanded clock offset. Real cross-sensor calibration and synchronization remain open.

## Layer sampling and physics

The M05 period-64 m optical and temperature fields are evaluated along world z at frame t. Stationary stochastic coefficient sets evolve with the accepted exact OU transitions. A separate deterministic transported profile holds coefficient amplitudes fixed and shifts their world position at 1 m/s. Temperature innovations use the environment seed plus 1,000,000. Each detector uses its own recorded frame seed, with the LWIR stream seeded one higher than RGB. Integration freezes the environment during each exposure, uses 16 equal-s slant segments, and evaluates z(s)=o_z+s d_z at each midpoint. The near-to-far sampling grid is traversed in reverse, and segments are serialized and consumed from surface to camera. Surface-to-camera integration length equals the ray's first-hit range. The homogeneous controls use one constant segment per ray. Layer boundaries, midpoint state, temperature and all optical terms are retained.

For RGB, the adapter applies the M05 power law independently to absorption and scattering referenced to 550 nm. The unchanged M02 kernel applies Lambertian surface radiance, attenuation from absorption and out-scattering, and isotropic in-scattering from prescribed illumination. For LWIR, M05 supplies different absorption coefficients at 8, 11 and 14 µm and a local temperature in kelvin. The unchanged M03 kernel applies wavelength-dependent surface emission/reflection, local thermal self-emission and ordered absorption-only transfer. Optical depth at wavelength λ is the sum of layer coefficient times slant thickness in m. The output fields named `rgb_tau550` and `lwir_tau11` are dimensionless **transmissions**, exp(−optical depth), at the stated wavelengths. M02 response uses 5 nm samples with fixed triangles. M03 uses 192 intervals across a synthetic 8–14 µm response band. M01 supplies the baseline slab convention, and its capture is covered by regression checks. Atmosphere and surfaces retain separate radiance terms.

Detector noise perturbs the common frame's two modal observations independently. M02 provides visible read noise, clipping and quantization, and M03 provides additive input-equivalent thermal noise and the detector rails. Radiance and codes are separate columns. The paired example displays actual detector codes. LWIR false color maps codes visually and has no temperature interpretation. The clipping control uses different detector rails, so full scale in its panels represents different input radiance.

## Distinctions and validity

First hit determines material before radiometry. A hidden target contributes no direct surface term. Thermal crossover can remove an LWIR intensity difference while target visibility and RGB contrast remain. Optical propagation can change contrast while geometry is fixed. Template axes and timestamps supply exact synthetic alignment. No measured scene, empirical opacity, real sensor calibration or generalization to physical weather is claimed. Single-scattering RGB, absorption-only LWIR, detector response, planar geometry, finite spectral quadrature, periodic and longitudinal-only fields, and the 16-segment approximation limit scope. No inference, forecasting or fusion is implemented. See the prior [governance register](../ACCEPTANCE_CONDITIONS_REGISTER.md) for external validation gates.
