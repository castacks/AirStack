# Structural identifiability and local information

For known incident radiance and path source, infer transmission by removing the source contribution from H=A+(B−A)T, provided B≠A. At known range L, κ=−log(T)/L is unique for 0<T≤1. If L is unknown, every pair (κ,L) with the same κL gives the same observation. In log-positive coordinates, the columns of the derivative with respect to log κ and log L match, and (1,−1) is a null direction. Two bands with independently unknown κ_RGB and κ_LWIR give rank two in three log parameters, with null direction (−1,−1,+1). A common known spectral relation or external range constraint changes the model and must be justified independently.

If the surface term is unknown, changing it can compensate attenuation through B=A+(H−A)/T. In a clear thermal band the mixture H=εB(T)+(1−ε)R has an emissivity–temperature tradeoff. Select a different T and set ε=(H−R)/(B(T)−R) where the result is admissible. The verification emits explicit matching examples. One modality may constrain a nuisance only with an independently justified relation to the other. Saturation, occlusion of the target, and thermal equality between boundary and effective path source can remove all local sensitivity to the target-path burden. A single-channel saturated or missing measurement is omitted.

In M06 RGB, the ratio of scattering to extinction and the in-scattering illumination are constant along the path. The identity for ordered affine slab transfer gives Hλ=Aλ+(Bλ−Aλ)exp(−L(k₀+a b)(550/λ)^p), where b is mean path burden. Known range, source and target reflectance make one scalar locally observable. LWIR under the same M06 conditions has variable atmospheric self-emission and an unknown temperature profile. Recovery with LWIR alone or extra joint evidence is outside that matched inverse. When the known-air M07 constraint holds, Hλ has the same affine-in-transmission structure with its own coefficients. Both modalities can inform the one scalar but joint rank remains one.

The diagnostic reports scaled Jacobians, singular values before noise whitening, and eigenvalues of the whitened Gram matrix using a declared Gaussian noise scale. Rank threshold is relative to the largest singular value. Two nonzero singular values are computed from row/column Gram invariants with a squared-minor determinant for numerical stability. Nonzero information can shrink sharply with atmospheric equilibrium or attenuation. Singular modes yield no finite observation-only covariance. Published intervals apply only to supported bounded-burden experiments.
