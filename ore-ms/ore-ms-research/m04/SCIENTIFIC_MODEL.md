# M04 camera geometry and geometric visibility

The camera is a zero-skew, undistorted pinhole with integer pixel-center coordinates. Camera axes are right-handed, with +x image-right, +y image-down and +z forward. The origin o is in metres in the world frame. R has the camera's three orthonormal axes as columns, expressed in world coordinates. The transform is p_c=Rᵀ(p_w−o). Intrinsics (fₓ,fᵧ,cₓ,cᵧ) are in pixels. For camera coordinates (X,Y,Z), the mapping is (cₓ+fₓX/Z,cᵧ+fᵧY/Z), for Z>0. Its camera-frame translation Jacobian has rows (fₓ/Z,0,−fₓX/Z²) and (0,fᵧ/Z,−fᵧY/Z²). The validation includes a translated, rotated camera and centered differences. [OpenCV pinhole model documentation](https://docs.opencv.org/2.4.13.7/modules/calib3d/doc/camera_calibration_and_3d_reconstruction.html)

For column u and row v, rotate and normalize the camera direction ((u−cₓ)/fₓ,(v−cᵧ)/fᵧ,1) into a world direction d. Then r(s)=o+sd and s is slant range in metres. An opaque rectangle is an axis-aligned bounded patch in a plane of constant world z, with x and y half-extents. Rectangles can translate with p(t)=p₀+vt using metres, metres per second, and seconds. For each candidate, solve s=(z_plane−o_z)/d_z and check closed world x/y bounds. Camera depth is s times the camera-frame forward direction component. Rays with |d_z|≤1e−12 or s<1e−8 m miss. Edges have zero positional padding. The solver resolves the nearest valid hit using a 1e−9 m tie group measured from the actual minimum range. Foreground precedes target in the group and lexicographic ID selects within the same priority. A selected hit may sit slightly behind the numerical minimum within that tie group. Unique IDs make enumeration irrelevant.

The target-only projection passes a ray through foreground to establish in-frame support independently of first-hit selection. V is one only where the target is the first hit. Frame visibility is N_visible/N_projected when N_projected>0. A missing fraction indicates empty in-frame support. No-hit pixels have blank first hit and ranges. A ray with foreground in front of the target records both target-only and foreground first-hit range. Foreground hits outside the projected target count as scene hits and do not enter the visibility numerator or denominator. The matrix uses integer-center binary coverage. Frame samples are instantaneous at configured times and imply no exposure integration or events between samples.

## Radiometric interface

Each valid first hit supplies its material ID and slant range to unchanged M02 RGB and M03 LWIR kernels. RGB materials have independently specified relative spectral reflectance and irradiance. LWIR materials carry temperature, emissivity and effective reflected temperature. The compatibility adapter uses separate synthetic homogeneous RGB and LWIR media in one control. The attenuated direct term and path term remain separate. The hidden target contributes neither direct emission nor direct reflection along the blocked ray. The adapter implements no indirect coupling between surfaces. Both modalities exercise noise-free digital readout and upper clipping. Geometry does not consume radiometric contrast, atmospheric transmission or code values. The matched-material arm is a deliberate zero-contrast example. It does not establish physical target detectability.

The supported primitive is a two-sided, opaque, planar rectangle of zero thickness and fixed world orientation. The work does not model transparency, volumetric blockers, diffraction, rotating or nonrigid primitives, subpixel antialiasing, stochastic motion, or real RGB–LWIR registration. The rational oracle exploits front-parallel fixture planes and independently projects corners. It is a reference for the frozen matrix, not a generic nonparallel renderer. The M02/M03 physical limits still apply to all radiometric results.
