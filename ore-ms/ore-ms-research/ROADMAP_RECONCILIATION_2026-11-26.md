## Infrastructure and experiment boundaries

R1 qualifies an operational Isaac/AirStack runtime, including PX4/MAVROS readiness, live stereo RGB, right depth and Ouster, named TF chains, and timestamp-specific sensor-to-map lookup. The nominal 30-second observer found 657 exact stereo pairs and all 48 sampled TF lookups. It also recorded sparse stereo disparity and stereo-cloud validity without a verified geometry reference. Foxglove R2 demonstrated advancing workspace WebSocket messages; the duplicate VDB advertisement has no demonstrated impact on ROS sensor capture or the proposed endpoint. The next study uses neither stereo disparity nor the VDB display. [Runtime qualification](isaac-readiness/RUNTIME_BASELINE_R1.md) · [Nominal observations](isaac-readiness/nominal-perception/README.md) · [Foxglove R2](isaac-readiness/FOXGLOVE_BROWSER_R2.md)

M01–M10 evidence is numerical or synthetic. M11 includes an independent simulation comparison and ambient Isaac readiness. A controlled Isaac geometry fixture would test known first-hit and projection behavior after its own approval and entry checks. The standing runtime can support noninvasive timing, data handling, and TF checks. It supplies no qualified LWIR stream, physical transmission label, exposure reference, or atmospheric ground truth. The fixture, even if successful, would not close those physical gaps. ## Recommended next milestone and experiment

The immediate governance action is disposition of M11-R1, followed by a separate M12 scope decision. The next scientific milestone is the existing **M12 Physical Sensor Verification**. Within it, select one stationary bench study of **two-second RGB path-transmission forecasts with and without LWIR conditioning** in one controlled stochastic attenuation regime.

This tests the incremental value of the second spectrum on independently measured physical outcomes, the distinction M08 could not establish in synthetic holdout. A matched joint-persistence arm separates multispectral observation from predictive dynamics. A single path, fixed target, one horizon and CPU scoring keep cost and operational risk below a geometry pilot or vehicle test. Reference instrumentation and support for the physical observation/dynamics model are the decisive dependencies. Without those inputs, the experiment is blocked. Repeating matched synthetic scores or measuring more nominal ROS telemetry cannot answer the physical transfer question. [Proposed protocol and execution gates](M12_STATIONARY_FORECAST_PROTOCOL.md)
