# Hurricane suburb on OSMO — session record, 2026-08-30/31

What was actually done, what was measured, what is fixed, and what is still
wrong. Written so the next agent does not repeat the four rounds of chasing
the water that this session spent.

The single most useful lesson is at the bottom, under **Method**.

---

## 1. Getting onto the pod

The pod is an OSMO dev workflow. Its entrypoint **clones AirStack fresh from
GitHub**, so the only code it sees is what is committed and pushed. Several
agents edit this working tree at once, so committing to ship one file drags
in everyone's in-flight work. Everything below therefore syncs an **explicit
file list** over the SSH port-forward instead.

```bash
airstack osmo:up          # submit the workflow (see osmo/workflows/airstack-dev.yaml)
airstack osmo:ide         # establishes the ssh port-forward on localhost:2200
```

Resource shape currently in `osmo/workflows/airstack-dev.yaml`:
1 GPU / 20 CPU / 120Gi RAM / 500Gi disk, `exec_timeout: 20h`.

> `osmo resource list` prints **USED/TOTAL**, not free/total. Misreading it
> as free capacity wasted a scheduling round.

### The tools (all in `scene_gen/tools/`)

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `osmo_sync.sh <paths…>` / `--dir <dir>` | push named working-tree files to the pod. No commit, no push. |
| `osmo_isaac.sh exec '<cmd>'` / `ssh '<cmd>'` | run a command in the sim container / on the pod host. **Resolves the container name** rather than assuming it. |
| `osmo_render_hurricane.sh <preset> <name> [ENV=v …]` | render one preset and pull its frames to `~/hurricane_previews/<name>/`. |
| `osmo_pull.sh <name> <dest>` | pull a snapshot dir. |
| `osmo_provision.sh` | wait for RUNNING, verify the clone, sync assets + code. |

**Container-name trap:** the sim container was renamed `isaac-sim-livestream`
→ `isaac-sim`; every hardcoded `docker exec isaac-sim-livestream` broke
silently. `osmo_isaac.sh` resolves it with
`docker ps --format '{{.Names}}' | grep -m1 isaac-sim`.

**`docker logs` is empty for this container.** Read the Kit log at
`/isaac-sim/.nvidia-omniverse/logs/<SNAPNAME>.log`.

`osmo_render_hurricane.sh` does **not** sync code — it runs whatever is on
the pod. Sync first, or you will benchmark the previous build. Verify with
e.g. `osmo_isaac.sh exec 'grep -c MARKER /isaac-sim/AirStack/…'`.

A render is **~2–3 minutes** end to end for the 500 m plate.

### Assets

`AIRSTACK_ASSET_ROOT` is **empty** in the sim container, so `airstack://`
resolves to the pod's own repo root (`/isaac-sim/AirStack`), not Nucleus.
Assets therefore have to be on the pod's disk; `osmo_provision.sh` rsyncs
the material/vegetation trees.

## 2. What was wrong, and how it was found

### 2a. The water wore a photograph of dry earth  ← the main regression

`WATER_DIFFUSE_TEXTURE` was `Soil_Mud/T_pjuph20_2K_B.png` — a photo of dry
cracked earth — bound as the water body's diffuse. Deep water is fully
opaque by construction, so past the shoreline the only thing on screen was
that photo at full strength, cracks and clods included. It is also the same
map bound to the pond bed and the silt overlay, so **the flood and the dry
ground wore the same texture and the waterline stopped existing.**

This produced two different-looking failures that were both this one cause:

- `FIX_L2`'s "saturated ochre sheet" = this map × a 2.25 tint.
- `OPQ_0p35`'s "dry basin" = this map at the *correct* tint.

`FINAL_L2` — the plate reviewed as best — looked like water precisely
because it had **no diffuse map at all**.

**Fix:** the water body binds no diffuse/normal/ORM texture. Its variation
comes from the sediment colour under a Fresnel coat, the procedural
`ripple_png` clearcoat normal (which has its own independent
`clearcoat_texture_scale`), and the per-band opacity. The **ponds keep**
their texture — a puddle is shallow enough to see the bed through, so silt
there *is* the bed.

### 2b. `chop` was being used as a microfacet roughness

`_make("Body_band…", chop, bump)` passed `chop` (a **wave steepness**,
sourced 0.10 for "flooded street, mild wind") straight into
`clearcoat_reflection_roughness`. At 0.10 the flood is a near-mirror and the
storm dome's sun came back as a **hard white disc blown out to pure white**,
tens of metres across.

**Fix:** `body_ccr = 0.18 + 0.9 * chop` → 0.27 at the sourced chop. Scoped
to the body; ponds keep their deliberate 0.03/0.05 mirror.

> Applies to the water BODY, i.e. the volume — now opt-in (§2d). At the
> shipped flat-quad default this is back to raw `chop`, which is what
> `FINAL_L2` used and which showed no disc under its own sky. **The disc
> risk returns with `SURGE_WATER_VOLUME=1`.**

### 2c. A full-weight clearcoat washed the water to pale grey

Measured with the water pass off and on: flooded pixels rendered
**RGB (172, 163, 153)** — a flat, colourless **+65 on all three channels**
over the mud bed beneath, when the sediment albedo (0.155, 0.115, 0.070)
should land near (108, 90, 70). That is the coat integrating the whole sky
dome as an ambient add. It is why the flood read as a **sand flat**: too
bright to be muddy, too desaturated to be brown, and in the overview within
6 levels of the dry ground beside it.

**Fix:** `_BODY_COAT_WEIGHT = 0.30` (water reflects ~2% at normal incidence
and the review cameras look near-nadir). After: **(157, 144, 130)**, and the
overview water-vs-dry contrast flipped from **+11.4 to −10.6** — the flood
is now darker than the dry ground, which is what makes a waterline read.

> Also volume-only, and also reverted to 1.0 at the shipped default (§2d).
> The measurement stands; it is the volume that is no longer on.

### 2d. THE VOLUME WAS REVERTED — the flat quad is the default again

**This is the state that ships.** `_use_legacy_flat_water()` now returns
True by default; the closed volume is opt-in behind **`SURGE_WATER_VOLUME=1`**
(`SURGE_FLAT_WATER=0` also works).

The volume is the better engineering and it lost anyway. It is a real closed
solid — true thickness, a ragged waterline from geometry rather than a
rasterised alpha map, depth driving opacity through Beer-Lambert instead of
being painted on. Every one of those is more correct than what the quad does.

But across five review rounds the volume was called, in order: a saturated
ochre sheet, a dry basin, a pale film with no waterline, and a sand flat —
while `FINAL_L2`, rendered with the flat quad *before any of that work*, was
picked as best every time the two were put side by side. The fourth such
comparison (`COMPARE_V2.png`) did not move the verdict.

Two of those four failures were the volume's own bugs and are fixed (§2a the
mud photograph, §2c the sky-dome wash). The residual is that a fully opaque,
near-uniform surface seen at a **grazing angle** reads as a mud slope
whatever colour it is, and the quad's alpha-map cutout dodges that by staying
partly transparent across its whole extent. Nobody has isolated that as a
defect; it is a look.

The volume is therefore **kept working and one env var away, not deleted** —
the depth grading is genuinely wanted and the oblique problem deserves
another pass.

> **If you re-enable the volume, restore two measured values with it** or you
> will rediscover both bugs from scratch. Both are recorded at their sites:
> `_BODY_COAT_WEIGHT = 0.30` (§2c) and `body_ccr = 0.18 + 0.9 * chop` (§2b).
> At the shipped defaults they are 1.0 and raw `chop`, which is what the flat
> quad has always used and what `FINAL_L2` rendered with.

### 2e. `d_opaque_m` 0.90 → 0.35

0.90 spread the depth ramp legibly but kept most of the flood thin enough to
see the bed through, which read as a pale film with no waterline. 0.35 is
what `FINAL_L2` used. The volume still earns its keep at 0.35: real
thickness, a ragged waterline from geometry rather than a raster, and the
whole 0–35 cm fringe graded.

### 2f. `SURGE=0` was silently ignored  ← this one cost a wrong conclusion

`surge.enabled()` exists and the launcher never called it. A control render
taken with `SURGE=0` came back all but identical to the water-on plate
(`deep_water_top` differed by **0.12/255**, no pixel off by more than 8),
which reads as overwhelming evidence that the water is invisible — and was
instead **two renders of the same scene**.

**Fix:** the launcher honours `SURGE=0` and raises a private
`_SurgeDisabled` caught *before* the generic handler, so a deliberate skip
is never reported as `WATER PASS FAILED`.

A valid control then showed water covering **37.1%** of the overview,
matching `coverage_frac 0.3745` exactly.

### 2g. The tint that was never connected (three call sites)

`planks.wood_material`, `vegetation.bark_material` and
`vegetation.split_wood_material` all set their `tint` on
**`diffuse_color_constant`**, which a bound `diffuse_texture` *replaces*.
The multiply slot is `diffuse_tint`. **Every `tint=` these functions were
ever passed was inert.** The evidence had been recorded twice without the
cause being found:

- `quake_flow.py:493` — a cedar tank tried at 0.50 and 0.085 rendered
  **pixel-identical**, concluded "the tint does not reach OmniPBR's diffuse",
  and gave up on a textured tank.
- `washaway.build_rafts` — reached past the function to set `diffuse_tint`
  on the shader itself.

Two independent workarounds for one unfixed primitive. `LOG_BARK_TINT` is
`(1.22, 1.10, 0.94)` — above 1.0 in two channels, which is only meaningful
as a multiply, confirming intent.

**Cross-pipeline effect, flagged deliberately:** fixing the primitive means
tints that were previously no-ops now take effect in the **tornado** and
**earthquake** pipelines too (e.g. `quake_flow`'s floor deck at
`(0.50, 0.41, 0.31)` will now actually darken). That is the authors' stated
intent, but those pipelines have not been re-reviewed since.

### 2h. Trees rendered healthy and green in a Cat-3 flood

`1,504 placed, 1,454 fell back to the green species USD (no archetype)`.
There was no hurricane tree archetype bake at all, so every damaged tree
rendered undamaged — the largest single lie in the scene.

## 3. What was built

### `scene_gen/tools/bake_hurricane_trees.py`  (new)

Offline — **no Isaac Sim, no physics, runs on a laptop in seconds** and
reproduces byte-for-byte.

It is not the tornado bake because `vegetation.WIND_LEVELS` has **no
`defoliated`**, and defoliated is the hurricane's plurality outcome (1,361
of 1,504 trees). It needs no solver because defoliating/thinning/leaning/
felling are pose-and-visibility changes to one rigid asset;
`vegetation.wind_tree` earns its settle by making *loose* debris, and
`bake-tree-and-debris` is explicit that such a settle does not reproduce.

- **Foliage is identified by bound MATERIAL name, not prim name.** A leaf
  card and a woody limb are both called `<Species>_branch4`; only the
  material separates them (`Shumard_Oak_leaf_v3_Mat2` vs `TreeBark_7`).
- `_FOLIAGE_WORDS` includes **`needle`** — Douglas_Fir binds `Pine_needles`
  and a `leaf`-only test reported it as 0% foliage, which would have made
  defoliation a silent no-op for the only conifer.
- Conifers keep most of their needles (sourced: broadleaf strips to 86–94%
  leaf loss at Cat 3+; needles present far less sail area).
- **`_cull(protect=1)` guards the trunk.** Several species carry their whole
  woody structure as ONE mesh, so `snapped` (keep 25%) hid the only woody
  prim and four species baked to an **empty Xform while reporting success**
  ("2 hidden of 2"). `_classify` returns woody largest-first so index 0 is
  the trunk.
- `lift` stays 0 at every level: rotating about the base keeps the trunk
  **planted**, which is what a viewer reads. A rigid 80° rotation also swings
  half a 25 m crown to −10.7 m; lifting until that cleared ground would put
  the root plate ten metres in the air. Terrain hides the buried half.

30 archetypes (6 species × defoliated/limbed/leaning/fallen/snapped), all
verified non-empty, heights 5.5–26 m, correctly in **metres** (sources are
centimetres; the launcher references archetypes at `scale = 1.0`).

Result: `trees 1504 placed, 0 fell back`.

> `snapped` is an **approximation** and is commented as such: a true snag
> needs the bole *clipped* at the break height, and trunk+limbs are one mesh
> so there is nothing to hide that leaves a stub. It strips foliage and 75%
> of the woody crown. It is there because the alternative was the rarest,
> most severe outcome rendering as the healthiest tree on the plate.

### Debris — `scene_gen/disaster/washaway.py`

Was **47 boxes** on a 500 m plate with 136 houses and 37% under water (3
timber, 3 sheet, 4 wall, 37 vegetation). Now **~712 pieces**.

- `raft_bg_per_100m2` 0.03 → **0.28**; `raft_per_house` 1.4 → **5.0**;
  `raft_min_depth_m` 0.5 → **0.25** (the waterline is where debris strands
  and is most visible).
- **`_RAFT_TANGLE`** — each cluster draw places 1–3 pieces within 0.7 m
  instead of one board. A raft field of evenly-spaced single planks is
  confetti at any density; flood debris arrives as interlocked mats.
- **`_house_mix`** — debris against a standing house skews to building
  material (organic capped at 20%). Measured: **72% building material**
  near houses, while open water stays vegetation-dominated.
- **New `log` class** — 2.5–8 m boles, 0.25–0.55 m through, round in section
  (`t == w`), riding proud of the surface with full roll. Bark tint
  `(0.105, 0.082, 0.058)`; without a `_RAFT_TINT` entry it would have
  rendered as a bright cream cylinder.

### Review cameras — `snapshots_rp.py`

`_clear_azimuth` rotates the oblique camera off a tree that would fill the
frame (12 m clearance, swept outward from the preferred bearing so lighting
is preserved, ±45° cap). Trees are handed in from the launcher via
`avoid=`. **Only the camera moves — the scene is unchanged.** Guarded with
`inspect.signature` because the viewport fallback module has no `avoid` and
passing it blind would lose every snapshot on exactly the path taken when
the preferred one already failed.

## 4. Where it stands

### The shipped configuration — UNRENDERED

Reverted on review, **2026-08-31**: the water is the **flat quad** (§2d),
`d_opaque_m` 0.35, clearcoat weight 1.0, coat roughness raw `chop`, no
diffuse texture on the body. Kept from this session's work: the 30 tree
archetypes (0 green fallbacks), ~712 debris pieces with floating logs, the
occlusion-aware review cameras, the `diffuse_tint` primitive fix, and a
working `SURGE=0`.

> **No render exists of this exact combination.** The revert was made without
> re-rendering, by explicit instruction. `FINAL_L2` is the nearest plate for
> the WATER (same quad, same knobs) but predates the trees and the debris;
> `V2_L2`/`V2_L3` have the trees and debris but were rendered with the
> VOLUME, which is now off. **Render before trusting either as
> representative.**
>
> ```bash
> ./scene_gen/tools/osmo_render_hurricane.sh suburb_hurricane_500_l2 <name> HUR_DOME_INT=1400
> ./scene_gen/tools/osmo_render_hurricane.sh suburb_hurricane_500_l3 <name> HUR_DOME_INT=1400
> ```

### Still wrong — the honest list

1. **The oblique cameras read the flood as a sand/mud slope.** This is the
   reason the volume was reverted (§2d) and it is unsolved rather than
   fixed — the flat quad avoids it, it does not answer it. Near-nadir the
   surface carries no sky colour, no horizon reflection, no metre-scale wave
   detail. Overviews work; obliques do not.
2. **L2 can produce no fallen trees at all.** `_TREE_CUTS` puts `leaning` at
   0.86 and `fallen` at 0.90, but the L2 field maxes at intensity 0.598 and
   even the ±0.26 jitter ceilings at 0.858 — three of six levels are
   **unreachable at L2**. The physically-right fix is a **saturated-soil
   windthrow boost**: trees standing in floodwater lose root anchorage, so
   local depth should raise the level. That also puts fallen trees exactly
   where they are wanted. **Not implemented.**
3. **One Hydra error persists**, on a single material:
   `parameter 'diffuse_texture': References an asset that can not be found:
   'airstack://…/Soil_Mud/T_pjuph20_2K_B.png'` — the *unexpanded* scheme
   reaching Hydra. The file exists on the pod and `_join_asset_root` resolves
   it correctly in isolation, so some call site authors the raw path. **Not
   located.** The water no longer binds that map; ponds/silt still do.
4. `snapped` trees are thinned crowns, not clipped boles (§3).
5. The **tornado and earthquake** pipelines have not been re-reviewed since
   the `diffuse_tint` fix (§2g) made previously-dead tints take effect.
6. The submerged-bed colour is `(0.30, 0.25, 0.18)`, lightened during the
   volume work to fix an inverted depth read. `FINAL_L2` had
   `(0.16, 0.13, 0.09)`. It was **not** reverted — it drives pond mud and
   deposits rather than the flat quad's own look — but it is a residual
   difference from the `FINAL_L2` plate and worth a look if the deposits
   read too pale.

## Method — the part worth keeping

**A large parameter change that produces a near-identical render means the
knob is not connected.** This session hit that signature five times:

- two lighting renders came back byte-identical → the camera was *inside*
  a tree (species USD is centimetres, referenced at 1.0);
- `d_opaque` 0.35 vs 0.55 identical on deep water → expected, deep water
  saturates either way (**not** a dead knob — checking this before acting
  avoided a wrong fix);
- dome 3000 vs 1400 barely moved the water → the water was not
  light-dominated, it had a milky cast of its own;
- a cedar tank at 0.50 and 0.085 rendered pixel-identical → the tint was on
  the wrong input (§2g);
- water-off vs water-on differed by 0.12/255 → **the off switch did
  nothing** (§2f).

The corollary, learned the expensive way: **verify the control before
trusting the conclusion.** The `SURGE=0` control was invalid and briefly
"proved" the water was invisible. One `grep` of the log for
`water_body(volume)` disproved it.

And: **measure, don't squint.** Depth-binned luma, per-channel means,
texture albedo, and wet-vs-dry pixel masks located every one of these; none
of them were visible as "the water looks a bit off".
